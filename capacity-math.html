Capacity planning — review note

The arithmetic behind maxUnavailable, corrected

Your three bullets are directionally right, but four refinements change the answer materially in practice — and one of them makes the HPA bullet wrong as written.

The formula people actually use
Available = Σ allocatable of the (N − maxUnavailable) LARGEST nodes in the pool − Σ DaemonSet requests on those nodes Required = Σ requests of all non-DaemonSet pods in the pool + maxSurge allowance for in-flight rollouts Utilisation = Required / Available → target ≤ 0.85

Four refinements

Each one moves the estimate in a specific, predictable direction — usually toward more risk than the naive sum suggests.

§1

Allocatable, not capacity

A 64 GiB node does not offer 64 GiB. Subtract systemReserved, kubeReserved, and the hard eviction threshold — typically 4–8 GiB and 1–2 cores on a bare-metal worker. If the customer never set KubeletConfig reservations, allocatable is optimistically high and the whole calculation is wrong in the dangerous direction.

§2

DaemonSets don't reschedule

Node exporter, OVN, CSI drivers, ACS collector, logging — they already run on every surviving node and consume allocatable there. So they come off the available side, not the required side. Counting them as pods needing a new home double-counts them, often by 3–5 GiB per node.

§3

Remove the largest nodes, not average ones

Bare-metal pools are rarely homogeneous. Worst case is losing your biggest node, so size against that.

§4

Bin packing, not just totals

Passing the sum test doesn't mean every pod fits. A single 24 GiB JVM needs one node with 24 GiB free, which five nodes with 20 GiB free each cannot provide. Check the largest single pod against the largest single node's remaining allocatable separately.

Worked example

The version to show a customer — six workers, 64 GiB / 32 vCPU, maxUnavailable: 1.

At 85% they hold maxUnavailable: 1 and have no room to go faster. At 110% the drain will stall with pods Pending — and on bare metal that means the pool sits half-upgraded. Two more nodes takes them to 68%, which buys maxUnavailable: 2 and roughly halves the wall-clock time of the window.

How to get the numbers

Per-node, from the CLI, when you're in front of one cluster.

Per node: allocatable and what is already requested
$ oc describe node bm-worker-01 | grep -A8 "Allocated resources"
Sum of memory requests per node, whole pool, in one pass
$ oc get pods -A -o json | jq -r '
.items[] | select(.spec.nodeName != null) |
.spec.nodeName + " " + ([.spec.containers[].resources.requests.memory // "0"] | join("+"))'
Are the reservations even set?
$ oc get kubeletconfig -o yaml | grep -A5 systemReserved

At fleet scale this is a Prometheus question, not a CLI one:

promql — requested vs allocatable memory, per node, excluding DaemonSets
sum by (node) (
kube_pod_container_resource_requests{resource="memory"}
* on(pod, namespace) group_left
(kube_pod_owner{owner_kind!="DaemonSet"})
)
/ sum by (node) (kube_node_status_allocatable{resource="memory"})

Graph that across the pool and the conversation stops being theoretical.

Correction — the HPA bullet

HPA doesn't just help — it inflates Required

The capacity check has to be done at maxReplicas, not at current replica count. A pool that sits at 70% today can be at 115% during a peak that coincides with the maintenance window.

1Size headroom against HPA maxima for anything customer-facing — not against whatever happens to be running right now.
2Keep minReplicas at or above the PDB floor, so scale-down can never leave a workload unable to tolerate an eviction.

It isn't that a drain removes a smaller share of capacity —
it's that with more replicas running, losing one replica is a smaller fraction of serving capacity, so the user-visible impact is smaller.

Different mechanism, better argument.
